The Leningrad trombone school

Dossier · Türner → Volkov → Kozlov → Venglovsky

The St. Petersburg–Leningrad school is one of the oldest lines of Russian brass playing. Its documented history begins in 1870, when the Austrian Franz Türner took over the trombone class of the St. Petersburg Conservatory. Direct teacher-to-pupil succession carried the tradition through a century, up to 1994. This reference dossier was compiled in November 2026 from the St. Petersburg Conservatory, the St. Petersburg Philharmonia and other open sources.

The “singing” manner

The common thread

The “Volkov method” grew out of Russian operatic singing: well-developed breathing, a singing tone and a clear, “bell-like” attack. Volkov passed these principles on to his pupils, Kozlov to his, and Venglovsky fixed them in his teaching manuals and repertoire editions.

1855–1909Türner

Origins: the orchestras of St. Petersburg and Franz Türner

Before the Conservatory opened, trombonists served in the Imperial theatre orchestras, in the court wind ensembles and in the bands of the Guards regiments; the backbone of the wind sections was made up of German, Austrian and Czech musicians brought in from abroad. In 1877 N. A. Rimsky-Korsakov, then inspector of naval bands, wrote a Concerto for trombone and military band for the Kronstadt sailors — the first Russian concert work for the instrument.

Franz de Paula Türner came to St. Petersburg in 1855 and played in the Imperial theatre orchestras for 32 years. He took part in the “Society of Amateur Wind Players” set up on the initiative of the future Alexander III; the tsarevich took tuba lessons from Türner. In 1870–1909 he taught the Conservatory’s trombone and tuba class. He became a Russian subject in 1887 and received personal nobility in 1888. Author of a “Practical Elementary School for All Brass Instruments” (1877) and an “Elementary School” (1878), reprinted until 1930. Pupils: P. N. Volkov, A. F. Kazbiryuk, the future Tallinn Conservatory professor K. Kreek, the conductor R. Kull.

1877–1933Volkov

Pyotr Naumovich Volkov

Born on 12 (24) January 1877 in the village of Golovchino, Kursk province, into the family of the serf horn player Naum Volkov, whose playing Tchaikovsky admired. He studied at the Kharkov branch of the Philharmonic Society, then at the St. Petersburg Conservatory with Türner (certificate 1901; diploma of “free artist” 1910, with the V. A. Schubert prize).

From 1899 until his death he was principal trombone of the Mariinsky (Kirov) Theatre orchestra, whose brass section was famous for its “organ-like” sound and was admired by Nápravník, Coates, Mottl and Cooper. In the 1926/27 season he played in the Philharmonic orchestra: on 27 March 1927, at the concert marking the centenary of Beethoven’s death, he took part in the Equali for four trombones.

At the Conservatory from 1906: senior teacher from 1909, professor 1917–1933; in 1930–1933 he also led the orchestral department of the workers’ faculty. Pupils: V. V. Kuznetsov, I. Z. Kheifets, P. A. Kurilov, I. A. Gershkovich, I. Z. Ivanov, P. V. Petrov, Ya. I. Raikhlin, B. I. Anisimov; Akim Kozlov began with Volkov at the workers’ faculty. According to the inscription on his trombone case, Jacob Raichman also graduated from Volkov’s class in 1911. Volkov died on 22 February 1933 in Leningrad.

1908–1992Kozlov

Akim Alekseevich Kozlov

Born on 9 September 1908 in the village of Yarygino, Smolensk province. In the famine years his parents placed him with a railway regiment: his bugle woke the regiment, the bandmaster noticed him, and he was taken into the band as a pupil. In 1931 the regiment sent him to the Leningrad Conservatory: the workers’ faculty with Volkov (1932–1933), then Reiche’s class (1933–1938) and postgraduate study (1941).

In 1937, still a student, he won the audition for the Leningrad Philharmonic orchestra — at the same time as E. A. Mravinsky arrived — and played in it for more than half a century. In 1941 he volunteered for the people’s militia but was recalled to the orchestra, which was leaving for evacuation in Novosibirsk. Mravinsky and Temirkanov valued his solos in Sibelius’s Seventh Symphony, Shostakovich’s Fifteenth and Ravel’s Boléro; Shostakovich thanked him for the solos in his symphonies. Natan Rakhlin called his section “an eagle flock of virtuoso trombonists”.

He taught at the Conservatory from 1946 (associate professor 1958, professor 1971); in 1972–1983 also at its Petrozavodsk branch. Pupils: V. Venglovsky, B. Vinogradov, A. Evtushenko, G. Danilov, G. Strautman — the core of the future Philharmonic trombone quartet. Honoured Artist of the RSFSR (1956), Order of the Badge of Honour (1983). On 26 March 1989, at eighty, Kozlov played Saint-Saëns’s Romance in the Small Hall with his daughter-in-law, the harpist Tatiana Tauer. He died on 22 November 1992.

⚠ Kozlov’s son, a double bass player in the orchestra, is named Sergei in the Philharmonia project and Boris in reference sources.

1926–1994Venglovsky

Victor Fyodorovich Venglovsky

Born on 17 May 1926 in Balta, Odessa region; orphaned early, he grew up in a children’s home. From 1940 a pupil of the Military Music School in Rostov-on-Don, from 1943 in the band of the Leningrad Military Transport Academy. Music college with I. Gershkovich (1945), Conservatory with Kozlov (from 1948, diploma with distinction 1953); in 1950 he won a place in the Philharmonic orchestra. In 1953 he took second prize at the festival in Bucharest.

Virtuoso soloist and leader of the Leningrad Philharmonic Trombone Quartet. On 23 April 1964 he gave the first Soviet performance of Milhaud’s Concertino d’hiver; in 1983, Purcell’s Music for the Funeral of Queen Mary. Works dedicated to him include a Concertino by V. Uspensky, “Metamorphoses” by G. Okunev, a Quartet by A. Smelkov and Three Pieces by V. Buyanovsky. His recordings were also released in Japan and the USA, among them the baritone trombone solo in Mahler’s Seventh Symphony under Kondrashin.

At the Conservatory from 1963 (associate professor 1972, professor 1984–1994). Pupils: A. N. Morozov, A. A. Skobelev (1st prize, Geneva 1973), V. V. Golikov. Author of the manual Daily Exercises for Trombone (1986); adviser to the Leningrad wind instrument factory when the first Soviet bass trombones were made. Member of the International Trombone Association (1976), Honoured Artist of the RSFSR (1991). He died on 28 March 1994.

⚠ When he began teaching at the Conservatory: the Conservatory’s collection and encyclopedia give both 1963 and 1966.
