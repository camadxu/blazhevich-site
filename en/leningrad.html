The Leningrad trombone school

Türner → Volkov → Kozlov → Venglovsky

The St. Petersburg–Leningrad school is one of the oldest lines of Russian brass playing. Its documented history begins in 1870, when the Austrian Franz Türner took over the trombone class of the St. Petersburg Conservatory. Direct teacher-to-pupil succession carried the tradition through a century, up to 1994. This reference page was compiled in November 2026 from the St. Petersburg Conservatory, the St. Petersburg Philharmonia and other open sources.

The St. Petersburg Conservatory on Theatre Square, archival photograph

A direct line of teaching spanning more than a century, from Türner’s arrival at the Conservatory (1870) to Venglovsky’s death (1994). As in Moscow, the root is foreign: Türner was Austrian; Volkov was the first Russian to lead the class.

The common thread

The “singing” manner

The “Volkov method” grew out of Russian operatic singing: well-developed breathing, a singing tone and a clear, “bell-like” attack. Volkov passed these principles on to his pupils, Kozlov to his, and Venglovsky fixed them in his teaching manuals and repertoire editions.

1855–1909Türner

Origins: the orchestras of St. Petersburg and Franz Türner

Before the Conservatory opened, trombonists served in the Imperial theatre orchestras, in the court wind ensembles and in the bands of the Guards regiments; the backbone of the wind sections was made up of German, Austrian and Czech musicians brought in from abroad. In 1877 N. A. Rimsky-Korsakov, then inspector of naval bands, wrote a Concerto for trombone and military band for the Kronstadt sailors — the first Russian concert work for the instrument.

Franz de Paula Türner (Franz Iosifovich; 21.04.1831, Gritza, Austria — 24.02 (9.03) 1909, St. Petersburg) came to St. Petersburg in 1855 and played in the Imperial theatre orchestras for 32 years. He took part in the “Society of Amateur Wind Players” set up on the initiative of the future Alexander III; the tsarevich took tuba lessons from Türner. He also played in court chamber music in the wind quartet/octet under V. V. Wurm and in concerts of the St. Petersburg Quartet Society. In 1870–1909 he taught the Conservatory’s trombone and tuba class: teacher 1870–1879 (listed as professor in IRMO reports), professor 2nd class 1879, professor 1st class 1881–1909. He became a Russian subject in 1887 and received personal nobility in 1888. Orders: St. Stanislaus 3rd class (1883), St. Anna 3rd class (1888), St. Stanislaus 2nd class (1896), St. Anna 2nd class (1905). Author of a “Practical Elementary School for All Brass Instruments” (1877) and an “Elementary School” (1878), reprinted until 1930. Pupils: P. N. Volkov, A. F. Kazbiryuk, the future Tallinn Conservatory professor K. Kreek, the conductor R. Kull.

Even before the Conservatory, the instrumental class of the Court Singing Chapel (from 1887) trained wind players in parallel; its teacher of trombone and tuba was Sergei Belkin (1836–1918). After the foreign musicians left in 1917, the Chapel kept Petrograd’s orchestras from losing their brass sections.

1877–1933Volkov

Pyotr Naumovich Volkov

Born on 12 (24) January 1877 in the village of Golovchino, Kursk province, into the family of the serf horn player Naum Volkov, whose playing Tchaikovsky admired. He studied at the Kharkov branch of the Philharmonic Society, then at the St. Petersburg Conservatory with Türner (certificate 1901; diploma of “free artist” 1910, with the V. A. Schubert prize).

From 1899 until his death he was principal trombone of the Mariinsky (Kirov) Theatre orchestra, whose brass section was famous for its “organ-like” sound and was admired by Nápravník, Coates, Mottl and Cooper. In the 1926/27 season he played in the Philharmonic orchestra: on 27 March 1927, at the Beethoven concert marking the centenary of the composer’s death (conducted by Malko and Glazunov), he took part in the “Equale” for trombone quartet — together with the Philharmonic orchestra players Mikhail Bogdanov, Pyotr Kurilov and Georgy Meshchanchuk.

At the Conservatory from 1906: senior teacher from 1909, professor 1917–1933; in 1930–1933 he also led the orchestral department of the workers’ faculty. Pupils: V. V. Kuznetsov, N. Z. Kheifets (1888–1942), P. A. Kurilov, I. A. Gershkovich, I. Z. Ivanov, P. V. Petrov, B. I. Anisimov (1907–1997); Akim Kozlov began with Volkov at the workers’ faculty. According to the inscription on his trombone case, Jacob Raichman also graduated from Volkov’s class at the Conservatory in 1911 — in the Boston Symphony Orchestra from 1927, its principal trombone in 1930–1955; a friend of Blazhevich and the first publisher of his studies in America. Volkov died on 22 February 1933 in Leningrad.

1908–1992Kozlov

Akim Alekseevich Kozlov

Born on 9 September 1908 in the village of Yarygino, Smolensk province. In the famine years his parents placed him with a railway regiment: his bugle woke the regiment, the bandmaster noticed him, and he was taken into the band as a pupil. In 1931 the regiment sent him to the Leningrad Conservatory: the workers’ faculty with Volkov (1932–1933), then Reiche’s class (1933–1938) and postgraduate study (1941).

In 1937, still a student, he won the audition for the Leningrad Philharmonic orchestra — at the same time as E. A. Mravinsky arrived — and played in it for more than half a century. In 1941 he volunteered for the people’s militia but was recalled to the orchestra, which was leaving for evacuation in Novosibirsk. Mravinsky and Temirkanov valued his solos in Sibelius’s Seventh Symphony, Shostakovich’s Fifteenth and Ravel’s Boléro; Shostakovich thanked him for the solos in his symphonies. Natan Rakhlin called his section “an eagle flock of virtuoso trombonists”.

He taught at the Conservatory from 1946 (associate professor 1958, professor 1971); in 1972–1983 also at its Petrozavodsk branch. Pupils: V. Venglovsky, B. Vinogradov, A. Evtushenko, G. Danilov, G. Strautman — the core of the future Philharmonic trombone quartet. Honoured Artist of the RSFSR (1956), Order of the Badge of Honour (1983). On 26 March 1989, at eighty, Kozlov played Saint-Saëns’s Romance in the Small Hall with his daughter-in-law, the harpist Tatiana Tauer. He died on 22 November 1992.

Kozlov’s son, the orchestra’s double bass player, is Boris Akimovich Kozlov (the “100 Years of the St. Petersburg Philharmonic” project, 100philharmonia.spb.ru/persons/40260: “the name of Mravinsky entered the life of Boris Kozlov in early childhood”). The variant “Sergei” appeared in reference sources; we keep “Boris” after the project page.

His section over the decades: Nikolai Korshunov, his fellow student in Reiche’s class (ZKR soloist 1937–1972, Conservatory professor, Sumerkin’s teacher), his pupil Georgy Danilov (in the orchestra 1947–1972, later the ensemble’s director), the bass trombone Alexei Evtushenko (1972–1995, later a conductor), Sumerkin’s successor at the leader’s desk Alexander Novakov (1976–2002) and Vladimir Lestov (1977–2020).

1926–1994Venglovsky

Victor Fyodorovich Venglovsky

Born on 17 May 1926 in Balta, Odessa region; orphaned early, he grew up in a children’s home. From 1940 a pupil of the Military Music School in Rostov-on-Don, from 1943 in the band of the Leningrad Military Transport Academy. Music college with I. Gershkovich (1945), Conservatory with Kozlov (from 1948, diploma with distinction 1953); in 1950 he won a place in the Philharmonic orchestra. In 1953 he took second prize at the festival in Bucharest.

Virtuoso soloist and leader of the Leningrad Philharmonic Trombone Quartet. On 23 April 1964 he gave the first Soviet performance of Milhaud’s Concertino d’hiver; in 1983, Purcell’s Music for the Funeral of Queen Mary. Works dedicated to him include a Concertino by V. Uspensky, “Metamorphoses” by G. Okunev, a Quartet by A. Smelkov and Three Pieces by Vitaly Buyanovsky. His recordings were also released in Japan and the USA, among them the baritone trombone solo in Mahler’s Seventh Symphony under Kondrashin.

At the Conservatory from 1963 (associate professor 1972, professor 1984–1994). Pupils: A. N. Morozov, A. A. Skobelev (1st prize and the special Ernest Ansermet Prize — “for perfect technique and depth of interpretation” — at the Geneva International Competition 1973; died in August 2011), V. V. Golikov. Author of the manual Daily Exercises for Trombone (1986); adviser to the Leningrad wind instrument factory when the first Soviet bass trombones were made. Member of the International Trombone Association (1976), Honoured Artist of the RSFSR (1991). He died on 28 March 1994.

Author of “Trombone School of the Petersburg-Petrograd-Leningrad Conservatory” // International Trombone Association Journal, 1984, Vol. XII, No. 2, pp. 26–28. Methodical works: “Foundations of Rational Embouchure” // Questions of Musical Pedagogy, issue 4. Moscow, 1983, pp. 57–63; “Specific Features of Tone Production…” (Gnesin Institute collection). Works written for and first performed by the Quartet: V. I. Kladnitsky, A. A. Boyarsky, G. G. Okunev, T. K. Mynbaev.

⚠ When he began teaching at the Conservatory: the Conservatory’s collection and encyclopedia give both 1963 and 1966.

The school today

Current trombone teachers at the St. Petersburg Conservatory: Dmitry Anatolyevich Andreev and Igor Yuryevich Yakovlev (Honoured Artist of the Russian Federation; the Conservatory’s profile lists him as professor, early descriptions of the department as associate professor).

The Mariinsky Theatre Trombone Ensemble: A. Gorbunov, S. Bogdanov, F. Romanov, V. Shcherbakov (concert poster 31.03.2026) — a direct heir of the trombone section of Volkov and Reiche in the same theatre.

ZKR ASO of the St. Petersburg Philharmonia: Dmitry Andreev — leader of the trombone section from 2003 (before that, 1996–2003, the Mariinsky Theatre orchestra). Maxim Ignatiev held that desk in 1989–2018; he is now in the orchestra of the Kapella (dossier below).

musicAeterna under Teodor Currentzis: Gérard Costes, Arman Surtaev, Andrey Saltanov, Vladimir Kishchenko; tuba — Ivan Svatkovsky.

The difference between the schools in legato (HBS, Yu. Gusev): Moscow (Blazhevich/Dikov/Sedrakyan) — “only by lips or the slide, without tongue or throat”; Leningrad (Reiche) — “possible the use of the tongue”.

1933–2019Sumerkin

Viktor Vasilyevich Sumerkin

Years and titles. Born 3 January 1933, died in the night of 12–13 May 2019. People’s Artist of the Russian Federation (decree No. 932 of 19 July 2004), Honoured Artist (1972); Order of the Badge of Honour (1981), Order of Honour (2009).

Studies. He graduated with distinction from the Rimsky-Korsakov Music College in 1952 (class of I. A. Gershkovich, a pupil of Volkov); from the Leningrad Conservatory — with distinction in 1957, class of Professor N. S. Korshunov; he completed his assistantship-internship in 1963 under M. N. Buyanovsky. In the year he graduated from the Conservatory he became a laureate of the Leningrad, Republican and All-Union competitions, and in August 1957 a laureate of the International Competition at the World Festival in Moscow.

Orchestra. From 1957 he played in the orchestra of the Kirov Theatre; from 1959 he was for about thirty years a soloist of the Leningrad Philharmonic Symphony Orchestra. His repertoire included concertos by David, Rimsky-Korsakov, Blazhevich, Nesterov, Spisak and Uspensky; recordings of Rachmaninov’s Prelude, Grechaninov’s Nocturne, Bozza’s Ballade and Anisimov’s Concert Etude with a wind orchestra.

Class. He taught at the college from 1956 and at the Conservatory from 1972: professor, head of the brass and percussion instruments department, chairman of the Council of Professors. From 1981 he ran the Leningrad trombonists’ seminars, and in 1995 the first international seminar, with the vice-president of the International Trombone Association taking part; he gave master classes in Finland (the Sibelius Academy, 1980 and 1982), Poland, the Netherlands, Germany and Luxembourg.

What he wrote. “Methods of Teaching the Trombone” (Moscow: Muzyka, 1987; 2nd ed., St. Petersburg, 2005) and the book “Trombone” (Moscow: Muzyka, 1975; St. Petersburg, 2005), articles on Volkov and on Blazhevich — in the Swiss Brass Bulletin and the English Trombonist; in 1992, at the congress of the International Trombone Association in Germany, he read a paper on the links between the Russian and German schools.

Dedications. A Concerto for trombone by D. Uber, a concerto for bass trombone by A. Nesterov, “Sumerkiana” for twelve trombones by A. Potienko, a concerto by O. Oblov written “for Viktor Sumerkin and Boris Vinogradov”.

Pupils and recognition. Among his pupils are R. Krasner, R. Galeev, V. Afonkin, M. Ignatiev, I. Yakovlev, V. Gorlitsky, A. Gorbunov, D. Andreev, A. Lobikov. The department reports a diploma as best professor of the International Trombone Association (2003). Buyanovsky about him: “a bearer of the finest traditions of the Leningrad-St. Petersburg trombone school, founded by P. Volkov, E. Reiche and V. Kuznetsov”.

Blazhevich in his work. Blazhevich’s concertos were in his repertoire, and the articles on Blazhevich in Brass Bulletin and Trombonist are the Leningrad school’s printed texts about the Moscow author. That Sumerkin studied from Blazhevich’s schools or studies is not shown by the sources.

1944–2012Vinogradov

Boris Parfentyevich Vinogradov

Years and titles. Born 11 December 1944 in Leningrad, died 30 July 2012 in St. Petersburg. Honoured Artist of the Russian Federation (2006), associate professor. Laureate of the international competition named after E. A. Reiche within the “Prague Spring” festival (1974, II prize); honorary diploma of the Ministry of Culture of the RSFSR (1982).

Studies. 1st Leningrad School of Military Music Cadets, baritone trombone class of A. A. Gladkov (1956–1963); the Conservatory, class of A. A. Kozlov (1970); assistant-intern under V. M. Buyanovsky (1974–1976).

Orchestras. Theatre of Musical Comedy (1966–1968), MALEGOTh (1968–1970), Kirov Theatre of Opera and Ballet (1970–1972), ZKR ASO of the Leningrad Philharmonic (1972–1994), then the St. Petersburg Academic Symphony Orchestra. He played in the Philharmonic brass quintet — in varying line-ups with the trumpeters V. Yu. Kafelnikov and M. B. Romanov, the horn players A. E. Glukhov and V. M. Buyanovsky, the tuba player V. V. Galuzin; and in the trombone quartet — with V. F. Venglovsky, A. T. Skobelev and A. M. Evtushenko.

Repertoire. Creston’s Sonata-fantasia, Hindemith’s Sonata No. 2, Bozza’s Ballade, Martinů’s Ballade, Blazhevich’s Concerto No. 5; one of the best performers of Serocki’s concerto, Matej’s sonata and Uspensky’s concertino. First performer in the USSR of a number of works — Albrechtsberger’s concerto (1769) and Schnittke’s “Schall und Hall” for trombone and organ (Leningrad, 24 February 1988). Recordings — Leningrad Radio and Melodiya (1973, 1976, 1978), including quartet programmes.

Teaching. Secondary Special Music School at the Conservatory (from 1977), music college at the Conservatory (from 1986), the Conservatory — teacher from 1976. In 1996 and 1998 he prepared two concerts in memory of Professor E. A. Reiche. His pupils work in orchestras in Russia and abroad, among them A. N. Repnikov, I. A. Bakanov, O. V. Svirchuk, Z. Kh. Alekbarov, M. E. Girin, E. G. Shishkin.

A discrepancy in the Conservatory’s reference: in the header the academic title of associate professor is dated 2007, in the text — 1978. Both dates are left as they are, without choosing one.

In the early version of the page the ensemble was called a “trombone quintet”, and the head of the assistantship — “M. N. Buyanovsky”. According to the Philharmonia’s databases and the Conservatory’s encyclopedia, Kafelnikov and Romanov are trumpeters, Glukhov a horn player, Galuzin a tuba player; the horn player of the brass quintet was V. M. Buyanovsky — son of the Mariinsky horn player M. N. Buyanovsky (he died in 1966, when Kafelnikov and Glukhov had not yet joined the orchestra). The initials of the assistantship have been corrected from “M. N.” to “V. M.” following the Conservatory’s reference.

1939–2007Strautman

Georgy Ivanovich Strautman

Years and titles. Born 21 March 1939 in Leningrad, died 29 January 2007. Honoured Artist of the Russian Federation (2001). Trombonist, conductor, teacher.

Studies and the theatre. In 1961 he graduated from the Leningrad Conservatory, class of Akim Kozlov, and in the same year joined the stage orchestra of the Kirov Theatre of Opera and Ballet. In 1969 he moved to the trombone section of the main orchestra, and from 1989 until the end of his life headed the theatre’s stage orchestra as conductor. Almost all the episodes with the stage wind band in the operas performed on the Mariinsky Theatre stage sound in his edition.

Teaching. The M. P. Mussorgsky Music College, the Secondary Special Music School-Lyceum at the Conservatory, Children’s Music School No. 11 of the Vasilievsky Island district (its history names him among those attracted by the first director); according to data for the 1990s — the N. A. Rimsky-Korsakov Music College. In 1967–1977 he headed the wind orchestra of the Leningrad Palace of Pioneers. He did not serve in the Philharmonic orchestra and did not teach at the Conservatory itself — the sources name only these places of work.

Pupils. Anatoly Skobelev (studied with him at Music School No. 11, later replaced him at the school as a teacher), Alexander Morozov (SSMS at the Conservatory), Vyacheslav Pachkaev (first trombone lessons), Alexei Repnikov (SSMS), Ivan Kryzhov (Rimsky-Korsakov College, graduated 1999), Mikhail Gurevich (Palace of Pioneers and the school on Sadovaya). Three more names — Nikolai Slepnev (tuba), Andrey Smirnov and Mikhail Seliverstov — are named as his pupils only in Wikipedia, with no external confirmation; they are set out in the general list with this reservation.

Editions. Compiler of “100 Studies for Trombone” (St. Petersburg: Kompozitor, books 1–2; the catalogue record is dated 1997, the imprint 1998) and of “The Trombonist’s Anthology. Concert Miniatures”.

ZKR 1937–1972Korshunov

Nikolai Sergeevich Korshunov

Service. Soloist of the orchestra of the Distinguished Ensemble — 35 years at one desk, 1937–1972. A native of Mologa; the Conservatory’s workers’ faculty (1933), course (1939), postgraduate study (1941). A pupil of E. A. Reiche — in one row with A. A. Kozlov, P. I. Kobzich and I. M. Polyatskin.

Class and recordings. From 1948 he taught at the Conservatory and ended his career as a professor; from his class came Viktor Sumerkin and the ZKR tuba player Valentin Galuzin. Serocki’s “Four Pieces” (1969) and Beethoven’s “Three Pieces” (1970) were recorded by the quartet with his participation — together with Venglovsky, Danilov and Kozlov.

For a long time he was not on the page: Wikipedia mentioned Korshunov’s class with a reference to Bolotin’s handbook, and the department’s page did not name him. The person is confirmed by the “100 Years of the St. Petersburg Philharmonia” database; his life dates are 1910–1989 according to the card of the same project (persons/10772); the month and day of birth have not been found in open sources.

b. 1968Yakovlev

Igor Yuryevich Yakovlev

Years and titles. Born 8 December 1968 in Leningrad. Honoured Artist of the Russian Federation (2005). Leader of the trombone section and soloist of the Mariinsky Theatre orchestra — from March 1992; Conservatory professor (associate professor in early descriptions of the department).

Studies. Rimsky-Korsakov Music College (1984–1988) and the Conservatory (1988–1996) — both in the class of Professor V. V. Sumerkin.

Orchestra. In 2000, at the personal invitation of Prince Charles, he played in a festive concert at Buckingham Palace — with Gergiev, Domingo, Leiferkus and Ulyana Lopatkina; in 2003 — in the World Orchestra for Peace project. He worked with the conductors Svetlanov, Temirkanov, Salonen, Boulez, Rostropovich and Maxim Shostakovich.

Ensemble and recordings. Founder and artistic director of the Mariinsky Theatre brass quintet: Grand Prix of the international competition of brass ensembles in Ulyanovsk (1999, the jury was headed by A. T. Skobelev), two CDs of the quintet (2002–2003: Ewald, Kutsir, Bernstein, Stravinsky — arrangements of “Pulcinella”, “The Firebird”, “The Rite of Spring”), tours from Israel and England to the USA and China. From 2013 — a Conn & Selmer artist.

Teaching. At the Conservatory from 2004; he also teaches at its ten-year music school (trombone and tuba). From 2014 — chairman of the jury of the “Silver Sounds” competition (Petrozavodsk). Among his pupils are laureates of international competitions, soloists of the Mariinsky Theatre. He collects historical documents about trombonists.

The Music House dates the All-Russian victory to 1991, the Conservatory profile — to February 1992 (Nizhny Novgorod); in the note on Sumerkin’s pupils’ victories the date 1991 is kept. The current official list of the Mariinsky Theatre orchestra is closed by an anti-bot check, so “to the present” is the wording of the Conservatory and the Music House.

b. 1968Ignatiev

Maxim Alekseevich Ignatiev

Years and titles. Born in April 1968 in Leningrad (the Conservatory’s website says 6 April, Wikipedia — the 7th). Honoured Artist of the Russian Federation (decree No. 236 of 22.02.2003).

Studies. Rimsky-Korsakov Music College (1987); the Conservatory (1992) and the assistantship-internship (1995) — class of Professor V. V. Sumerkin.

Orchestra. ASO of the Philharmonia (December 1988 — April 1989), then, by competition — leader of the trombone section of the ZKR ASO: he held the desk for almost twenty years, until 2018; from 2018 — the orchestra of the State Academic Kapella of St. Petersburg under A. Chernushenko. He played under Temirkanov, Jansons, Gergiev, Rozhdestvensky, Svetlanov, Solti, Muti and Giulini.

Competitions. First prizes of the All-Russian Competition (Leningrad, 1988) and the All-Union Competition (Minsk, 1988); first prize of the International Competition in Nizhny Novgorod (1994).

Soloist. The Philharmonia’s chronicle has preserved his concerts: Michael Haydn with the horn player S. Dovgalyuk and the ZKR (1991), David’s Concertino with the ZKR (1994), Albrechtsberger (1994), Launy Grøndahl, Uspensky and Tomasi (1997), Rimsky-Korsakov (2000, 2004), Trojan’s “Frog Serenade” (30–31.12.2015); orchestral solos — Shostakovich’s Fourth and Fifteenth Symphonies, Mahler’s Third, “Scheherazade”.

Ensemble and recordings. From 1997 — trombone of the Philharmonic brass quintet “Russian Brass”; with it the disc of Mussorgsky’s “Pictures at an Exhibition” was recorded (2004).

Teaching. From 2002 he teaches at the Rimsky-Korsakov College, from 2007 — at the Conservatory; he also ran a class at the Herzen Russian State Pedagogical University. Master classes in England, Holland, Germany and the USA, at “Sirius” (Sochi); jury member of All-Russian and international competitions.
