About this project

Transparency and sources

An independent project about the life, music and teaching legacy of Vladislav Blazhevich. It does not represent the Moscow Conservatory, the libraries or the performers it links to. This version has no registration, donations or competition entries. The Russian version is the primary one; this English page follows it. Bracketed source numbers here match the English list below, not the Russian one.

Русская версия →

The opening image is an artistic composition supplied by the site owner: Blazhevich with his trombone in front of the Great Hall of the Moscow Conservatory and the Bolshoi Theatre; it is not a historical photograph. The portrait in it is an AI-generated artistic image based on a 1903 photograph of Blazhevich (Wikimedia Commons, public domain; source listed on Commons: A. G. Kharlamov, ITA Journal 36/3, 2008, pp. 30–34), toned to match the archive photographs. It is not a historical photograph. The buildings follow photographs of the Tchaikovsky Moscow Conservatory in the 1940s and the Bolshoi Theatre around 1905 (public domain).

Sources

Twelve sources grouped by subject. The numbers match the bracketed references in the text; they do not match the Russian numbering.

Life and biography

  1. Moscow Conservatory, history of the brass department and Bork’s personal file (Russian).
  2. Wikipedia: Vladislav Blazhevich and list of compositions; the early life cites A. G. Kharlamov, “Blazhevich: His life and work,” ITA Journal 36/3 (2008), 30–34.
  3. Krugosvet encyclopedia (Russian). the original domain is parked; the text was read in the Web Archive (snapshot 15 May 2025). The Bolshoi, Persimfans and chair dates match [1], [2] and [7]. The Goznak factory band (from 1920) appears in the archived copy and is not confirmed by the other sources read. The same copy dates the concertos 1934–1940, which contradicts [2] and [6]; that dating is not used in the text.
  4. A. Shtilman, In the Bolshoi Theatre and the Metropolitan Opera (fragment on Raichman, Russian). The Cyclowiki list of St. Petersburg Conservatory teachers is no longer online (domain suspended; no archive snapshot). As of 8 November 2026 Volkov was absent from that list.

Works and editions

  1. A. Kharlamov, The History of Selected Works and Destiny of Works in the West (2009); chapters of Kharlamov’s dissertation (Northwestern University, 2005).
  2. C. Lambert, The Solo Trombone Works of Vladislav Blazhevich, University of Iowa, 2020; Blazhevich Recording Project.

Schools and teachers

  1. Wikipedia (Russian): Bork, Reiche, Korshunov; Saint Petersburg Conservatory encyclopedia: A. Kozlov, V. Venglovsky; class of 1942 (Russian).
  2. Gnesin Music College, history of the Moscow wind school; classON (Russian).

Raichman and Boston

  1. D. Yeo, “Joannès Rochut…”, ITA Journal, January 2025, pp. 74 ff., notes 159–194 (based on the Boston Symphony Orchestra archive and an interview with Raichman’s daughter, 1994); The Last Trombone, 2020; The Last Trombone, January 2025.
  2. J. M. Thompson, review of “School for Trombone in Clefs” (East-West), ITA Journal 39:1, 2011, with a list of American editions; Online Trombone Journal — review of “Before Blazhevich”; library record of “Studies in Clefs”, IMC, 1957. read 8 November 2026

Moscow orchestra · illustrations

  1. Vechernyaya Moskva: E. Pyatunina, V. Bogdanov, “Trumpeters”, 17 October 2012; E. Pyatunina, “The orchestra of our memory”, 9 November 2017 (Russian). Read 9 November 2026.
  2. Illustrations (Wikimedia Commons): the 1903 photograph with autograph (the basis of the portrait; the photograph itself is in the biography), the Bolshoi Theatre, about 1905, the Moscow Conservatory, 1940s. The Moscow school page header is a collage of the last two shots against a painted square; the buildings were not photographed side by side like that.
