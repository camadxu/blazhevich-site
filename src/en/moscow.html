15th–19th c.Origins

How the trombone came to Russia

The trombone’s ancestor is the sackbut (Burgundy, 15th century). By the 17th century the instrument had taken almost its modern form; solo playing flourished in the first half of the 19th century (F. Belcke, K. Queisser, Ferdinand David’s Concertino).

In 18th-century Russia the trombone was seen as a “church, Catholic” instrument, and the low brass in orchestras was often replaced by the horn band. According to one study, even in the St. Petersburg performance of Haydn’s Creation (1801) the trombone parts were played on horns ⚠. By the publication of Kozlovsky’s Requiem (1826) the horn band had given way to a group of three trombones — from then on the trombone became a regular orchestral instrument in Russia. For a long time there were no native brass players: musicians were recruited abroad.

1859–1916Bork

Christoph Bork: where the Moscow school begins

Khristofor Ivanovich Bork (Christoph Borck; 7 July 1832, Havelberg, Prussia — 8 (21) March 1916, Moscow). Chronology after his personal file in the Russian State Archive of Literature and Art, RGALI (fond 659, op. 3, item 454), and an article by A. I. Ivanov (2021):

  • December 1859 — petition to admit a “Prussian subject” to the Bolshoi Theatre orchestra to play the ophicleide; refused.
  • 28.04.1861 — permission to join the orchestra, this time as a trombonist; 31.10.1861 — oath of Russian citizenship.
  • 1861–1906 — service at the Bolshoi Theatre: until 1882 principal trombone of the ballet orchestra, then of the opera too; from 1867, because of illness, moved to timpani; from 1889 played percussion only.
  • 1866 — a trombone and tuba class opens at the Moscow Conservatory; the first brass teachers are Bolshoi Theatre players M. Barthold (horn), F. Richter (trumpet) and Kh. Bork (trombone).
  • 01.01.1886 — Order of St. Stanislaus, 3rd class.

⚠ Discrepancies: Wikipedia and Bolotin’s dictionary give his title of supernumerary professor as 1875 and full professor as 1886; according to RGALI (Ivanov), supernumerary professor 2nd class from 1883 and 1st class from 1906. The Conservatory’s website gives the years of his class as 1875–1916.

Bork drew up a “Programme of the trombone class (slide trombone in B♭)” — a manuscript in RGALI (fond 2099, op. 1, ff. 121–125), the first systematic trombone course in Russia. At the time the principal subject had one academic hour a week, and a class had two or three pupils. His pupils included V. M. Blazhevich and V. Gushchin; Bolotin’s dictionary adds K. M. Kupinsky, V. V. Derzhanovsky, S. Diev, I. Piorkovsky, A. Sobolevsky and S. Chesnokov ⚠.

1928Questionnaire

Blazhevich in his own words

From the questionnaire Blazhevich filled in on leaving the theatre in 1928 (personal file, copy in the Glinka Museum): born on 3 August 1881 in Smolensk province, Katyn district, on the Trigubovka estate; Roman Catholic, son of a tenant landowner; reads, writes and speaks Polish and German. In 1900 he entered Bork’s class and in 1905 graduated from the Conservatory with the title of “free artist”; principal trombone of the Bolshoi Theatre in 1906–1928. In 1909–1911 he served as a musician in the band of the Alexander Military School, and in 1917–1918 as a warden of the 9th Combined Evacuation Hospital. During the war he declined to be evacuated with the Conservatory and stayed in Moscow.

Blazhevich’s collection in the Glinka Museum holds an undated “List of students in Prof. Blazhevich’s class”: first thirteen trombonists, among them V. A. Shcherbinin and B. P. Grigoriev, then the tuba players; each with attendance in per cent and the professor’s comment, signed at the bottom “Professor /Blazhevich/”.

People

18.01.1896, Maikop — 05.06.1963, Moscow

Vladimir Arnoldovich Shcherbinin

A legend of the Moscow school, founder of the “Shcherbinin method”.

  • 1914–1918 — studied and played in a regimental band; from 1918 in the Red Army, as musician and bandmaster.
  • Moscow Conservatory, Blazhevich’s class: 1924–1930, or as an external student until 1947 ⚠.
  • 1926–1944 — soloist of the Bolshoi Theatre orchestra; 1926–1932 — member of Persimfans, the conductorless orchestra.
  • 1929–1940 — led the wind band of the Moscow Automobile Plant’s club (AMO, later ZiL), a “forge of musicians”.
  • 1938–1941 — also soloist of the USSR State Wind Band; 1943–1951 — its director and artistic director; from 1953 — director of the Moscow Philharmonic orchestra.
  • 1935 — prizewinner at the 2nd All-Union Competition of Performing Musicians; 02.06.1937 — Honoured Artist of the RSFSR.
  • Teaching: from 1938 at the Moscow Conservatory, associate professor from 1939, professor of trombone and tuba from 1947 until his death; in 1950–1952 associate professor at the Gorky Conservatory (where Ladilov studied with him).
  • 1947 — a massed wind band of 1,200 players for Moscow’s 800th anniversary and the 30th anniversary of the October Revolution.
  • Method: a “special system of daily practice” (R. Maslov). The rule of the class: the pupil learns the notes alone, the teacher works on form, phrasing and nuance.
  • Awards: two Orders of the Badge of Honour (1946, 1959), the Medal for Labour Valour (1949). Buried in the Novodevichy Cemetery.
  • Arranger: transcriptions of Italian and Russian music for trombone and piano, including Rachmaninoff’s Cello Sonata.
09.02.1927, village of Achka (now Sergachsky district, Nizhny Novgorod region) — 13.02.1983

Konstantin Mikhailovich Ladilov

  • Soloist of the Gorky Opera and Ballet Theatre orchestra 1947–1955.
  • 1952 — graduated from the Gorky Conservatory in Shcherbinin’s class; postgraduate study at the Moscow Conservatory completed in 1956.
  • 1st prize at the 4th World Festival of Youth and Students, Bucharest, 1953 (a festival, not a competition).
  • From 1957 until 1983 — soloist and principal trombone of the USSR State Symphony Orchestra under Svetlanov.
  • Teaching: Gorky Music College 1949–1959, Gorky Conservatory 1952–1964, Gnesin Institute from 1964, associate professor from 1979. At entrance auditions he asked for an F major scale over four octaves.
  • Pupils: V. Kh. Shkolnik, I. Bogolepov, I. Mordovsky, V. I. Fedorinov and others. A. A. Nesterov’s Trombone Concerto was written for Ladilov and dedicated to him.
  • Honoured Artist of the RSFSR (1979).
  • Buried at the Vagankovo Cemetery (plot 35).
b. 07.06.1937, Ramenskoye

Viktor Borisovich Batashev

  • ⚠ The surname is given as on his Moscow Conservatory page: Batashev (Баташёв). Wikipedia and some discographies spell it Batashov.
  • 1956 — Moscow Conservatory music college; 1960 — Moscow Conservatory, Shcherbinin’s class; 1962 — assistantship.
  • 1958 — 1st prize at the Geneva International Competition: the first Soviet wind player to win a first prize at an international competition in a “capitalist country”; 1962 — 3rd prize at the Prague Spring.
  • 1959–2001 — soloist and principal trombone of the Grand Symphony Orchestra (now the Tchaikovsky Symphony Orchestra).
  • Teaching at the Moscow Conservatory since 1963, professor from 1995; Honoured Artist of the RSFSR (1981).
  • Key performances: the solo in Berlioz’s Grande symphonie funèbre et triomphale with the orchestra under G. Rozhdestvensky (1968); Milhaud’s Concertino d’hiver; Schnittke’s “Sound and Echo”.
  • Pupils: V. Kiselev, A. Pavlov, D. Tolpegov, A. Zhigankov, K. Aleksandrov, D. Sharov, M. Starshinov and others.
b. 1924

Mikhail Nikolaevich Turusin

  • Moscow Conservatory music college 1944–1947; Moscow Conservatory 1947–1952, Shcherbinin’s class.
  • Soloist of the Bolshoi Theatre orchestra 1948–1959 and 1963–1975; Radio and Television Symphony Orchestra 1960–1963.
  • 1st prizes at the World Festivals of Youth and Students — Budapest 1949 and Berlin 1951.
  • Moscow Conservatory from 1965 (associate professor 1970, professor 1990); author of trombone curricula (1988, 2012). RGALI, fond 648 (Bolshoi Theatre), op. 13, item 790: “M. N. Turusin, b. 1924, orchestra artist, 31.01.1963–16.07.1975” — confirms the second period of his service at the Bolshoi Theatre.
  • Honoured Cultural Worker of the RSFSR — decree of 2 December 1991.

Dmitry Mikhailovich Piguzov

  • Graduated from the Rimsky-Korsakov Leningrad State Conservatory in 1964, according to the State College of Wind Music faculty list and the archived State Academic Symphony Orchestra biography. Neither source names his teacher.
  • People’s Artist of the Russian Federation — Decree No. 962 of 2 September 2006.
17.02.1917, Baku — 1993

Mamed Mirzoevich Zeinalov

  • Azerbaijan Conservatory 1937–1940; Moscow Conservatory, Shcherbinin’s class, graduated in 1947 (Chernykh’s reference book gives 1949 ⚠).
  • USSR State Symphony Orchestra 1947–1949; soloist (bass trombone) of the Bolshoi Theatre 1949–1979; Honoured Artist of the RSFSR (1978).
  • Moscow Conservatory from 1963 (associate professor 1978, professor 1984).
  • The “Trombone Method” written with A. N. Sedrakyan (1987, reissued 2001 and 2025) carries on Shcherbinin’s teaching.
12.08.1911, Odessa — 26.03.2001, Moscow

Other pupils of Shcherbinin

  • Nikolai Ivanovich Mironov (1928–2017) — Moscow Conservatory 1958, Shcherbinin’s class; Cinematography Committee orchestra 1956–1978; Geneva 1958 — 2nd prize: together with Batashev, a double success for the Soviet school.

The Gnesin branch

After O. A. Makarishin’s article “A History of the Trombone Class of the Gnesin Institute — Gnesin Academy”.

  • Boris Petrovich Grigoriev (1906, Tver) — Bolshoi Theatre soloist 1929–1960, Blazhevich’s class; from 1944 taught trombone and tuba at the Gnesin Institute. His “Scales, Arpeggios and Intervals for Trombone” (1969) became “the trombonist’s handbook”. Pupils: Chepkoi, Lokalin, Dosadin, Zheleznov, Maltsev, Levashkin, Zdorov, Moreinis.
  • Nikolai Grigorievich Filippov (b. 1930) — principal trombone of the Moscow Philharmonic orchestra 1962–1985 (under Kondrashin); at the Gnesin from 1983, professor 1995.
  • Viktor Afanasievich Lebedev (b. 1953) — Gnesin Institute (Batashev’s class, 1984); taught at the Gnesin 1988–1999.
  • Vladimir Khaimovich Shkolnik — Gnesin Institute 1978 (Ladilov’s class); 1st prize at the All-Union Competition (Tallinn, 1980); from 1985 in the USSR State Symphony Orchestra under Svetlanov, principal trombone from 1991.
  • Oleg Arkadievich Makarishin — Gnesin Institute 1997 (Filippov’s class); teaching at the Gnesin since 2001.
  • Aleksandr Nikolaevich Morozov — Leningrad Conservatory (Venglovsky’s class); Kirov (now Mariinsky) Theatre Orchestra 1976–1978, then, invited by A. T. Skobelev, soloist of the Bolshoi Theatre trombone section 1978–2013; at the Gnesin since 2004.
  • Ilya Vladimirovich Geller — Gnesin Institute 1996 (Filippov’s class); CD “Penta of time”.
  • Vladimir Nikolaevich Dosadin (tuba) — pupil of Grigoriev, soloist of the Grand Symphony Orchestra; from 1973 the first teacher of the Gnesin tuba class.

Biographies

The masters of the school: in brief

These notes were compiled on 16 November 2026 from open sources. Where the sources disagree, this is stated in the text rather than smoothed over. On 18 November they were supplemented with information from the site owner — the masters’ Leningrad period and A. T. Skobelev’s invitation to the Bolshoi Theatre.

  1. Anatoly Tikhonovich Skobelev (1946–2011) — a native of Leningrad, graduated from the Leningrad Conservatory in 1973 and in the same year received the 1st prize and the Ernest Ansermé special prize (“for perfect technique and depth of interpretation”) at the Geneva International Competition; in 1974–1990 he played in the Bolshoi Theatre orchestra, in 1990–2003 he was a soloist of the Russian National Orchestra. He taught at the Moscow Conservatory from 1979 and headed the brass instruments department from 2007. People’s Artist of the Russian Federation (1997).
  2. Evgeny Pavlovich Nesterenko (1937–2011) — studied in Leningrad; he began working at the Bolshoi Theatre orchestra before Skobelev — as the site owner notes, it is by no means simple for a musician trained in Leningrad to get into the Bolshoi. Honoured Artist of the Russian Federation, taught at the Gnesin Music College. His years of service at the Bolshoi Theatre are not given in the sources; the year and place of birth are unconfirmed — the date is taken from the heading of an obituary.
  3. Valery Vitalyevich Golikov (b. 1951, Vologda) — studied at the Leningrad Conservatory from 1971 with Akim Kozlov and Viktor Venglovsky. After the Conservatory — the Kirov Theatre orchestra (now Mariinsky), then, invited by A. T. Skobelev, moved to Moscow: artist of the Bolshoi Theatre orchestra, later of the Russian National Orchestra; deputy principal trombone of the National Philharmonic Orchestra of Russia. Honoured Artist of the Russian Federation (2006).
  4. Vyacheslav Pavlovich Pachkaev (1945–2021) — graduated from the Leningrad Conservatory in the class of Akim Kozlov. From 1975 in the Kirov Theatre orchestra, then, invited by A. T. Skobelev, at the Bolshoi Theatre; bass trombonist of the Russian National Orchestra from 1990. Taught at the Gnesin school, made arrangements for bass trombone.
  5. Aleksandr Nikolaevich Morozov (1946–2016) — graduated from the Leningrad Conservatory in 1980 (class of Viktor Venglovsky). In 1971–1976 played in the jazz orchestras of Joseph Weinstein and Eddie Rosner, in 1976–1978 at the Kirov Theatre, then, invited by A. T. Skobelev, from 1978 until the end of his life soloist of the Bolshoi Theatre orchestra. Taught at the Gnesin Academy from 2004.
  6. Grigory Khersonsky (Vul) (b. 1937) — graduated from the Moscow Conservatory in 1961 (class of Petr Chumakov), winner of the 1st prize at the 1963 All-Union Competition. Soloist of the All-Union Radio and Television Symphony Orchestra (1962–1969), the USSR State Symphony Orchestra (1969–1983), from 1990 of the Haifa Symphony Orchestra. He worked at neither the Mariinsky nor the Bolshoi Theatre; the patronymic, date and place of birth differ between sources.
  7. Konstantin Mikhailovich Ladilov (1927–1983) — soloist of the Gorky Opera and Ballet Theatre orchestra (1947–1955) and the USSR State Symphony Orchestra (1957–1983). Graduated from the Gorky Conservatory in 1952 in the class of Vladimir Shcherbinin; taught in Gorky and at the Gnesin Institute. Honoured Artist of the RSFSR (1979; in the body of the Wikipedia article — 1978).
  8. Mikhail Nikolaevich Turusin (b. 1924) — class of Vladimir Shcherbinin at the Moscow Conservatory (1947–1952). Soloist of the Bolshoi Theatre orchestra (1948–1959 and 1963–1975), the Radio and Television Symphony Orchestra (1960–1963). Teaching at the Moscow Conservatory from 1965, professor from 1990; his personal collection is kept in RGALI. “Honoured Cultural Worker of the RSFSR” — decree of 2 December 1991.
  9. Dmitry Mikhailovich Piguzov (1938–2022) — bass trombonist of the Svetlanov State Academic Symphony Orchestra of Russia, People’s Artist of the Russian Federation (Decree No. 962 of 2 September 2006). Graduated from the Rimsky-Korsakov Leningrad State Conservatory in 1964, according to the State College of Wind Music faculty list and the archived State Academic Symphony Orchestra biography. These sources do not name his teacher. From 1987 he taught at the military conductors’ faculty of the Moscow Conservatory.

Teaching legacy

What the “Shcherbinin school” means

  1. Shcherbinin left no printed method of his own. His legacy is a “special system of daily practice” and the living practice of his class.
  2. The continuation — the “Trombone Method” by M. M. Zeinalov and A. N. Sedrakyan (Muzyka, 1987; reissued 2001 and 2025).
  3. The foundation — the methods and etudes of Blazhevich.
  4. Grigoriev — “Scales, Arpeggios and Intervals for Trombone” (1969), etudes, collections of orchestral excerpts.
  5. Turusin — trombone curricula (1988, 2012).

The school today

1946–2011Skobelev

Anatoly Tikhonovich Skobelev

Born on 16 April 1946 in Leningrad, died on 19 August 2011 in Moscow. People’s Artist of Russia (1997), Honoured Artist of the RSFSR (1988). Graduated from the Leningrad Conservatory in 1973.

Before moving to Moscow he played in the Leningrad Philharmonic trombone quartet together with V. F. Venglovsky, B. P. Vinogradov and A. M. Evtushenko.[on the quartet]

In 1974 he joined the Bolshoi Theatre orchestra and played there for sixteen years, until 1990. In 1990–2003 he was a soloist of the Russian National Orchestra and led the “RNO Low Brass Section” ensemble.

The Geneva victory became the bridge to Moscow: the 1973 prize was followed by a place in the Bolshoi Theatre orchestra. He strengthened the section with his Leningraders — on Skobelev’s invitation Valery Golikov, Vyacheslav Pachkaev and Aleksandr Morozov moved to the Bolshoi from the Kirov (now Mariinsky) Theatre.

His pupils in Moscow’s present-day orchestras: in Skobelev’s biography on the Moscow Conservatory website, among his pupils — RNO soloists — Dmitry Zheleznov is named, now principal trombone of the Bolshoi Theatre; Ivan Irhin graduated from the Conservatory in Skobelev’s class (RNO biography); Yuri Kolosov studied with Skobelev according to the official TSO biography. ⚠ According to the site owner, Skobelev’s pupils also included Valery Sushchenko and Pavel Gaidai-Turlov (Bolshoi Theatre trombone section) and Erkin Yusupov, now a professor at the Moscow Conservatory.

He taught at the Moscow Conservatory from 1979 — associate professor, then professor; in 2007 he took over the brass and percussion department and headed it until the end of his life. His pupils include the tuba player Yegor Pavlov.

His biography joins two lines: a Leningrad Conservatory education and a Moscow department. No information about an official page of the festival named after him has been found in open sources, so the name of the festival is not given here as an established fact.

According to the Moscow Conservatory department (N. A. Tokarev’s dissertation, Rostov, 2020), the trombone class is currently taught by V. B. Batashev, A. V. Starkov and E. B. Yusupov. The Conservatory’s English page: Erkin Yusupov — Professor, Acting Department Head (Trombone); Viktor Batashyov — Professor, Honored Artist of the RSFSR.

Yegor Pavlov (b. 1982, MGASO): studied with A. T. Skobelev (tuba, Moscow Conservatory), A. N. Morozov, V. B. Batashev and V. V. Sumerkin; sackbut internship in the UK — a living example of the two schools crossing.

Erkin Yusupov: in the Bolshoi Theatre orchestra from 2003, principal trombone; teaching from 2006, now the Moscow Conservatory trombone class (see above). ⚠ According to the site owner, he has recently left the Bolshoi Theatre orchestra and concentrated on teaching: in March 2025 the Bolshoi announced a competition for the vacant place of a soloist in the trombone section.

Trombone sections today: Philharmonic, TSO, RNO

The school lives on in Moscow’s present-day orchestras. Yuri Kolosov studied with Mikhail Turusin and Anatoly Skobelev; Ivan Irhin began with Konstantin Ladilov, finished the Gnesin school with Vyacheslav Pachkaev and the Conservatory with Anatoly Skobelev; Dmitry Sharov is a pupil of Viktor Batashev. Blazhevich’s name today. The site owner: in Russia the name of Blazhevich is undeservedly being forgotten. Not long ago Children’s Music School No. 80, which bore his name, was closed — its graduates work in many of Moscow’s leading ensembles. We have not found reports of the closing of School No. 80 in open sources; the wave of “reorganisations” of Moscow children’s art schools was written about at the end of 2024, and that it was precisely Children’s Music School No. 80 that bore Blazhevich’s name is confirmed by trade publications of 2019 — links in the sources.
