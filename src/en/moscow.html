15th–19th c.Origins

How the trombone came to Russia

The trombone’s ancestor is the sackbut (Burgundy, 15th century). By the 17th century the instrument had taken almost its modern form; solo playing flourished in the first half of the 19th century (F. Belcke, K. Queisser, Ferdinand David’s Concertino).

In 18th-century Russia the trombone was seen as a “church, Catholic” instrument, and the low brass in orchestras was often replaced by the horn band. According to one study, even in the St. Petersburg performance of Haydn’s Creation (1801) the trombone parts were played on horns ⚠. By the publication of Kozlovsky’s Requiem (1826) the horn band had given way to a group of three trombones — from then on the trombone became a regular orchestral instrument in Russia. For a long time there were no native brass players: musicians were recruited abroad.

1859–1916Bork

Christoph Bork: where the Moscow school begins

Khristofor Ivanovich Bork (Christoph Borck; 7 July 1832, Havelberg, Prussia — 8 (21) March 1916, Moscow). Chronology after his personal file in the Russian State Archive of Literature and Art, RGALI (fond 659, op. 3, item 454), and an article by A. I. Ivanov (2021):

  • December 1859 — petition to admit a “Prussian subject” to the Bolshoi Theatre orchestra to play the ophicleide; refused.
  • 28.04.1861 — permission to join the orchestra, this time as a trombonist; 31.10.1861 — oath of Russian citizenship.
  • 1861–1906 — service at the Bolshoi Theatre: until 1882 principal trombone of the ballet orchestra, then of the opera too; from 1867, because of illness, moved to timpani; from 1889 played percussion only.
  • 1866 — a trombone and tuba class opens at the Moscow Conservatory; the first brass teachers are Bolshoi Theatre players M. Barthold (horn), F. Richter (trumpet) and Kh. Bork (trombone).
  • 01.01.1886 — Order of St. Stanislaus, 3rd class.

⚠ Discrepancies: Wikipedia and Bolotin’s dictionary give his title of supernumerary professor as 1875 and full professor as 1886; according to RGALI (Ivanov), supernumerary professor 2nd class from 1883 and 1st class from 1906. The Conservatory’s website gives the years of his class as 1875–1916.

Bork drew up a “Programme of the trombone class (slide trombone in B♭)” — a manuscript in RGALI (fond 2099, op. 1, ff. 121–125), the first systematic trombone course in Russia. At the time the principal subject had one academic hour a week, and a class had two or three pupils. His pupils included V. M. Blazhevich and V. Gushchin; Bolotin’s dictionary adds K. M. Kupinsky, V. V. Derzhanovsky, S. Diev, I. Piorkovsky, A. Sobolevsky and S. Chesnokov ⚠.

1928Questionnaire

Blazhevich in his own words

From the questionnaire Blazhevich filled in on leaving the theatre in 1928 (personal file, copy in the Glinka Museum): born on 3 August 1881 in Smolensk province, Katyn district, on the Trigubovka estate; Lithuanian, Roman Catholic, son of a tenant landowner; reads, writes and speaks Polish, Lithuanian and German. In 1900 he entered Bork’s class and in 1905 graduated from the Conservatory with the title of “free artist”; principal trombone of the Bolshoi Theatre in 1906–1928. In 1909–1911 he served as a musician in the band of the Alexander Military School, and in 1917–1918 as a warden of the 9th Combined Evacuation Hospital. During the war he declined to be evacuated with the Conservatory and stayed in Moscow.

⚠ In the questionnaire Blazhevich calls himself Lithuanian, while according to Kharlamov his father came from the Polish nobility.

Blazhevich’s collection in the Glinka Museum holds an undated “List of students in Prof. Blazhevich’s class”: first thirteen trombonists, among them V. A. Shcherbinin and B. P. Grigoriev, then the tuba players; each with attendance in per cent and the professor’s comment, signed at the bottom “Professor /Blazhevich/”.

People

18.01.1896, Maikop — 05.06.1963, Moscow

Vladimir Arnoldovich Shcherbinin

A legend of the Moscow school, founder of the “Shcherbinin method”.

  • 1914–1918 — studied and played in a regimental band; from 1918 in the Red Army, as musician and bandmaster.
  • Moscow Conservatory, Blazhevich’s class: 1924–1930, or as an external student until 1947 ⚠.
  • 1926–1944 — soloist of the Bolshoi Theatre orchestra; 1926–1932 — member of Persimfans, the conductorless orchestra.
  • 1929–1940 — led the wind band of the Moscow Automobile Plant’s club (AMO, later ZiL), a “forge of musicians”.
  • 1938–1941 — also soloist of the USSR State Wind Band; 1943–1951 — its director and artistic director; from 1953 — director of the Moscow Philharmonic orchestra.
  • 1935 — prizewinner at the 2nd All-Union Competition of Performing Musicians; 02.06.1937 — Honoured Artist of the RSFSR.
  • Teaching: from 1938 at the Moscow Conservatory, associate professor from 1939, professor of trombone and tuba from 1947 until his death; in 1950–1952 associate professor at the Gorky Conservatory (where Ladilov studied with him).
  • 1947 — a massed wind band of 1,200 players for Moscow’s 800th anniversary and the 30th anniversary of the October Revolution.
  • Method: a “special system of daily practice” (R. Maslov). The rule of the class: the pupil learns the notes alone, the teacher works on form, phrasing and nuance.
  • Awards: two Orders of the Badge of Honour (1946, 1959), the Medal for Labour Valour (1949). Buried in the Novodevichy Cemetery.
  • Arranger: transcriptions of Italian and Russian music for trombone and piano, including Rachmaninoff’s Cello Sonata.
09.02.1927 — 13.02.1983

Konstantin Mikhailovich Ladilov

  • 1952 — graduated from the Gorky Conservatory in Shcherbinin’s class; postgraduate study at the Moscow Conservatory (1955 or 1956 ⚠).
  • 1st prize at the International Competition in Bucharest (1952 or 1953 ⚠).
  • From 1955/1957 ⚠ until 1983 — soloist and principal trombone of the USSR State Symphony Orchestra under Svetlanov.
  • Teaching: Gorky Music College 1949–1959, Gorky Conservatory 1952–1964, Gnesin Institute from 1962/1964 ⚠. At entrance auditions he asked for an F major scale over four octaves.
  • Pupils: V. Kh. Shkolnik, I. Bogolepov, I. Mordovsky, V. I. Fedorinov and others. A. A. Nesterov’s Trombone Concerto was written for Ladilov and dedicated to him.
  • Honoured Artist of the RSFSR (1978).
b. 07.06.1937, Ramenskoye

Viktor Borisovich Batashev

  • ⚠ The surname is given as on his Moscow Conservatory page: Batashev (Баташёв). Wikipedia and some discographies spell it Batashov.
  • 1956 — Moscow Conservatory music college; 1960 — Moscow Conservatory, Shcherbinin’s class; 1962 — assistantship.
  • 1958 — 1st prize at the Geneva International Competition: the first Soviet wind player to win a first prize at an international competition in a “capitalist country”; 1962 — 3rd prize at the Prague Spring.
  • 1959–2001 — soloist and principal trombone of the Grand Symphony Orchestra (now the Tchaikovsky Symphony Orchestra).
  • Teaching at the Moscow Conservatory since 1963, professor from 1995; Honoured Artist of the RSFSR (1981).
  • Key performances: the solo in Berlioz’s Grande symphonie funèbre et triomphale with the orchestra under G. Rozhdestvensky (1968); Milhaud’s Concertino d’hiver; Schnittke’s “Sound and Echo”.
  • Pupils: V. Kiselev, A. Pavlov, D. Tolpegov, A. Zhigankov, K. Aleksandrov, D. Sharov, M. Starshinov and others.
b. 1924

Mikhail Nikolaevich Turusin

  • Moscow Conservatory music college 1944–1947; Moscow Conservatory 1947–1952, Shcherbinin’s class.
  • Soloist of the Bolshoi Theatre orchestra 1948–1959 and 1963–1975; Radio and Television Symphony Orchestra 1960–1963.
  • 1st prizes at the World Festivals of Youth and Students — Budapest 1949 and Berlin 1951.
  • Moscow Conservatory from 1965 (associate professor 1970, professor 1990); author of trombone curricula (1988, 2012).
17.02.1917, Baku — 1993

Mamed Mirzoevich Zeinalov

  • Azerbaijan Conservatory 1937–1940; Moscow Conservatory, Shcherbinin’s class, graduated in 1947 (Chernykh’s reference book gives 1949 ⚠).
  • USSR State Symphony Orchestra 1947–1949; soloist (bass trombone) of the Bolshoi Theatre 1949–1979; Honoured Artist of the RSFSR (1978).
  • Moscow Conservatory from 1963 (associate professor 1978, professor 1984).
  • The “Trombone Method” written with A. N. Sedrakyan (1987, reissued 2001 and 2025) carries on Shcherbinin’s teaching.

Other pupils of Shcherbinin

  • Nikolai Ivanovich Mironov (1928–2017) — Moscow Conservatory 1958, Shcherbinin’s class; Cinematography Committee orchestra 1956–1978; Geneva 1958 — 2nd prize: together with Batashev, a double success for the Soviet school.
  • Mikhail Akimovich Izmalkov (b. 1933) — trombone solo at the first performance of Shostakovich’s restored Fourth Symphony on 30.12.1961; soloist of the Moscow Philharmonic orchestra.
  • Aleksandr Viktorovich Rybintsev (d. 2008) — bass trombone and bass trumpet, soloist of the Bolshoi Theatre orchestra 1979–2005; the theatre’s electronic archive lists 23 productions, from Das Rheingold (1981) to The Golden Age (2008).

The Gnesin branch

After O. A. Makarishin’s article “A History of the Trombone Class of the Gnesin Institute — Gnesin Academy”.

  • Boris Petrovich Grigoriev (1906, Tver) — Bolshoi Theatre soloist 1929–1960, Blazhevich’s class; from 1944 taught trombone and tuba at the Gnesin Institute. His “Scales, Arpeggios and Intervals for Trombone” (1969) became “the trombonist’s handbook”. Pupils: Chepkoi, Lokalin, Dosadin, Zheleznov, Maltsev, Levashkin, Zdorov, Moreinis.
  • Nikolai Grigorievich Filippov (b. 1930) — principal trombone of the Moscow Philharmonic orchestra 1962–1985 (under Kondrashin); at the Gnesin from 1983, professor 1995.
  • Viktor Afanasievich Lebedev (b. 1953) — Gnesin Institute (Batashev’s class, 1984); taught at the Gnesin 1988–1999.
  • Vladimir Khaimovich Shkolnik — Gnesin Institute 1978 (Ladilov’s class); 1st prize at the All-Union Competition (Tallinn, 1980); from 1985 in the USSR State Symphony Orchestra under Svetlanov, principal trombone from 1991.
  • Oleg Arkadievich Makarishin — Gnesin Institute 1997 (Filippov’s class); teaching at the Gnesin since 2001.
  • Aleksandr Nikolaevich Morozov — Leningrad Conservatory (Venglovsky’s class); soloist of the Bolshoi Theatre trombone section 1978–2013; at the Gnesin since 2004.
  • Ilya Vladimirovich Geller — Gnesin Institute 1996 (Filippov’s class); CD “Penta of time”.
  • Vladimir Nikolaevich Dosadin (tuba) — pupil of Grigoriev, soloist of the Grand Symphony Orchestra; from 1973 the first teacher of the Gnesin tuba class.

What the “Shcherbinin school” means

Teaching legacy
  • Shcherbinin left no printed method of his own. His legacy is a “special system of daily practice” and the living practice of his class.
  • The continuation — the “Trombone Method” by M. M. Zeinalov and A. N. Sedrakyan (Muzyka, 1987; reissued 2001 and 2025).
  • The foundation — the methods and etudes of Blazhevich.
  • Grigoriev — “Scales, Arpeggios and Intervals for Trombone” (1969), etudes, collections of orchestral excerpts.
  • Turusin — trombone curricula (1988, 2012).

Freely available scores

  • IMSLP — works by Blazhevich: Concertos No. 2 and No. 5, concert duets, sequences, etudes.
  • National Electronic Library — “School for Slide Trombone”, 1925: the original edition.
